CyberGrill 14 - the real deck, the real animation, three languages, and voice
============================================================================

THIS ONE NEEDS A SERVER DEPLOY. The Worker changed again (voice signalling
and the "does this count" flag), so after update.ps1:

    cd server
    npx wrangler deploy
    cd ..

Or just run deploy14.ps1, which does the whole lot.

THE CARDS
  Redrawn from your photographs, suit by suit. The batons are long tapered
  clubs with three cut branch-stubs, leaning out from the middle of the face
  and alternating red and blue the way they are printed. The swords have the
  blue blade, the gold crescent guard, the hatched red grip and the stacked
  pommel, and they alternate point-up and point-down. The coins are gold with
  a ring of teeth biting inwards and an eight-pointed star struck in the
  middle; the ace carries its red ribbon. The cups are built like little
  drums - red lid, hatched gold barrel, blue petalled foot, red plinth. The
  sota, caballo and rey are proper figures now, standing on their strip of
  gold ground, holding their suit.

  The pintas are right too: oros has an unbroken frame, copas one break a
  side, espadas two, bastos three. That is how you read a hand fanned in
  somebody's fist.

THE ANIMATION
  Two beats instead of one. The card leaves the hand and lands ON the cards
  it is about to take - which is the moment that makes a capture make sense,
  you see the seven come down on the seven - and then, after a breath, the
  whole little stack is swept off to whoever took it.

  And the fix you asked for: head to head, the other player's captures used
  to fly into YOUR hand. The seats had no pile for the animation to aim at,
  so it fell back to yours. Every seat has one now, and so do you - there is
  a card count beside your hand showing what you have taken.

FRENCH AND ARABIC
  All three tables. A row of flags on the setup screen and a chip on the
  table, and the choice follows you from the scoreboard into a game and back.
  Arabic lays the whole table out right to left, but not the cards - a hand
  reads left to right in every language.

  Ronda is translated all the way down: every line of commentary, every log
  line, the calling prompts, the round card. The suits keep their Algerian
  names in Arabic - dhab, kouba, chbada, kabouya - and the calls keep theirs
  in all three: a darba is a darba in Algiers whatever you are speaking.

THE VOICE ROOM
  A microphone chip on any online table. Everyone who joins hears everyone
  else, directly browser to browser - the server only carries the
  introductions, never the audio. There is a bar along the bottom with who is
  in, a green ring round whoever is talking (on their seat as well), a level
  meter, mute, and the music steps back while somebody is speaking.

  Honest limit: there is a STUN server but no paid TURN relay, so on a
  network that refuses direct connections - some mobile carriers, some office
  firewalls - the voice will not come through even though the game does.

THE SCOREBOARD
  Online games can now count. When you make a room there is a switch in the
  lobby - COUNTS ON THE SCOREBOARD or FRIENDLY, NOT COUNTED - and the host
  decides. If it counts, the result waits in this browser and lands on the
  scoreboard the next time you open it, with the players matched by name and
  anybody new added. The board also knows Ronda and Ludo as games now, which
  it did not before.

FIXED ALONG THE WAY
  * The online room used UNO's 500 as the Ronda target instead of 41.
  * Nine chips ran off the edge of a phone; the row wraps now.
  * The end-of-round count line lost the third player behind "A and B".

Verified in a real browser: the full 40-card deck at table size, two-, three-
and four-handed solo games, a three-player online round from the deal to the
count, two browsers in a voice room actually connecting, all three tables in
all three languages, results landing on the scoreboard, and a 390px phone.
